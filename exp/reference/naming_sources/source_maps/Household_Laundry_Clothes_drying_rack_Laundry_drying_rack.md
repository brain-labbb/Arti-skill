category_scope: One freestanding household laundry drying rack that stands on the floor and folds. Every used record in the pool is the same machine: a single rigid horizontal spine (a long top rail plus paired hinge rails and a lower towel rail, roughly 1.76 m long and 0.78-1.08 m off the floor) that is the assembly root, two or more folding leg frames hinged to that spine on transverse axes and reaching the floor, and one or more drying wings hinged or slid off the spine's side rails so the rack opens out to a much wider drying span than its folded footprint. The spine-plus-folding-supports relation and the wing-off-the-spine relation are the recognisability-critical features and are invariant across every used record, so they are template core rather than slot candidates. In scope: any floor-reaching support geometry that hinges to that spine (crossed scissor legs, converging A-legs with a floor cross bar, vertical end posts on a splayed base), any ground contact at the leg tips (rubber caps, swivel casters, skid bars), any wing surface that hangs on the spine's side rail (parallel clothes bars, a woven mesh grid shelf, a comb of clip fingers, a single plain extension arm), either wing kinematics present in the pool (a fold-up REVOLUTE hinge or a telescoping PRISMATIC slide along the spine), the small hangers and clip carriers the reference photographs show hanging off the top rail, and spine-mounted accessory rungs. Out of scope and excluded from the pool: ceiling-suspended pulley airers (no floor support at all — the spine hangs from a ceiling on a lift line), wall-mounted bracket racks (the spine is a wall plate and the rack has no legs), and rigid four-post tower racks with fixed stacked shelves (no folding leg mechanism; the whole host topology changes rather than one component).

  because no record instantiates a hanger at all; see the deviation note below. The station rule is the
  template's: hangers are spaced evenly along the usable run of the spine top rail, derived from the rail
  length and the selected hanger's own body width so neighbours cannot touch at full sway.

The largest declared combination is 3 leg frames + 4 wings (2 tiers x 2 sides) + 6 hangers + 6 caster wheels
(2 tips x 3 frames) on one spine = 20 parts and 19 joints, inside the fleet's 19-part p90 and well under the
33-part maximum. No source-built count is truncated by that budget: the pool's own maxima are 3 leg frames,
4 wings, 9 bars and 4 casters, all reachable.

## Deviations from source construction

Recorded here because they change how a cited span is reproduced.

* **The hanger is promoted from dead code, and two further forms come from the bound photographs.**
  `_hanger_geometry` (origin L106-L134) is defined in all seven used records — together with a
  `HANGER_MATERIAL` — and called by none of them; `build_object_model` never creates a hanger part in any
  record in the pool. The reference photographs the subcategory is anchored on show hangers as a dominant
  feature (loaded shirt hangers along the top rail in 001.png, hangers plus a multi-clip sock cluster in
  002.png), so the owner's decision on 2026-08-01 was to make the hanger a real slot with three candidates.
  `wire_triangle` reproduces the cited span exactly and only adds the part, joint and rail pivot the source
  omitted. `clip_trouser_bar` and `sock_clip_ring` keep that span's hook sweep, stock radius and rail pivot
  and change the body below the hook to the two other carriers visible in the bound photographs; they have no
  independent source record. This is a deliberate departure from `AUTHORING.md` §3's preference that a new
  structural candidate first exist as a reviewable source asset, taken with the owner's explicit approval.
* **`a_frame_legs`, `post_base_legs`, `comb_clip_wing`, `single_rail_arm`, `skid_bar_feet`,
  `shoe_cradle_rung` and `towel_hoop_rail` are likewise photograph-derived**, built on the cited source
  construction for their slot (the leg frame helper, the wing boundary frame, the leg lower cross tube, the
  spine rail and end cross member respectively) with the geometry the reference photographs show. Same
  provenance caveat as above and the same owner decision. Every one of them keeps its slot's source interface
  unchanged — the leg hinge axis and station, the wing hinge rail, the leg tip station, the spine rail
  construction — so the full cartesian product still builds without a compatibility gate.
* **`ctx.allow_overlap` is not carried over.** Every used record wraps its mechanism interfaces in it: the
  wing inner tube declared coaxial with the fixed hinge rail (origin L318-L324), the folding leg top tube
  declared captured inside the plastic hinge block (origin L326-L334), the mid-span leg through the centre
  hinge plate (`leg_support_count` L346-L356), and each caster against its leg frame
  (`caster_wheels` L367-L378). That call is blocked for Design-backed templates. The template instead gives
  each of those interfaces a real running fit: the wing's inner tube runs in an open hinge block whose bore is
  sized off the tube radius, the leg's top cross tube likewise, and each caster is carried on its own joint
  clear of the leg tube. Where a member is genuinely carried by its joint rather than resting on the host,
  `ctx.allow_isolated_part` is used, which is the sanctioned escape and is not blocked.
* **The caster wheel is not built with `WheelGeometry`.** `var_caster_wheels` L161-L195 uses
  `WheelGeometry(radius, width, rim=..., hub=..., bore=WheelBore(style="round", ...))`. Every bored
  `WheelGeometry` solid tessellates into **two** geometry components, measured on 2026-08-01 across
  this template's whole declared caster band (radius 0.014-0.038 m, with and without the rim flange
  and the bead seat, and with and without an added hub bushing): the second component sits 0.06-2.1 mm
  from the first and no external bridge closes it. `fail_if_part_contains_disconnected_geometry_islands`
  (1e-6 m, intra-part) rejects that, and the source's own record passes only because its author tests
  never ran the check. A caster this small is a solid disc in reality, so the template welds one:
  a running tread cylinder plus a proud hub boss on both faces, in a single merged mesh. Everything
  else about the mechanism is kept - a separate part per leg tip, a CONTINUOUS joint on the rolling
  axis through the wheel centre, the wheel radius equal to the height the leg gives up, and a real
  fork on the leg that straddles the wheel and reaches its hub on two stub axles instead of the
  source's `allow_overlap` between wheel and leg tube.
* **Joint limits are derived, not copied.** The source's fixed 0.95 rad leg fold, -0.55..0.85 rad wing swing
  and 0.45 m telescope stroke are read as evidence that these ranges exist, not as constants. The template
  derives the leg fold from the leg length and the spine height so the feet never rise above the spine, the
  wing swing from the wing's own reach so a fully folded wing lies alongside the spine instead of through it,
  and the telescope stroke from the spine length so the source's rail-engagement invariant holds at full
  extension for every spine size.
* **The scissor brace is a slot, not a fixture.** The origin builds it unconditionally (L281-L295) and
  `wing_count` deletes it to clear its lower wing tier. Rather than tie its presence to the tier count — which
  would be a compatibility gate — the template makes it one of three spine accessories and routes it above the
  lower tier's swept envelope, so `scissor_brace` combines with `wing_tier_count = 2`.
