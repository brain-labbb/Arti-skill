category_scope: One manually operated bench citrus/juice press. Every member of the pool is the same machine: a rigid standing frame whose base carries a circular drip deck, a removable deep collector cup seated on that deck, a removable perforated strainer nested in the cup mouth, exactly one guided ram that runs on a vertical PRISMATIC stroke through a bore in the frame's upper beam and ends in an integral bell/cone press head that descends into the cup, and exactly one operator actuator mounted on that upper beam which drives the ram. The ram-into-cup relation and the perforated strainer plate are the recognisability-critical features and are invariant across the pool, so they are template core rather than slot candidates. In scope: any frame topology that presents an upper beam with a ram bore over a base-mounted deck (twin-post gantry and single-column C-arch are both in the pool), any base support (feet on a bench, or a C-clamp bracket gripping a bench edge), and any hand actuator that resolves to one vertical ram stroke (lever + dog-link, rack + pinion side crank, screw + top handwheel). Out of scope: powered/centrifugal juicers (a separate subcategory with a motor and a spinning basket), squeeze-handle hand reamers with no standing frame and no guided ram, and citrus reamers or strainers sold without a press mechanism — each drops either the guided vertical ram or the frame that carries it.

## Multiplicity evidence and what is not carried over

`base_support_count` (N = 3..6, item slot `mount_style`) rests on two index-general rules, one per
candidate, both read off the sources above:

* `corner_foot_pads` — the origin anchor's `for index, (x_pos, y_pos) in enumerate(...)` loop
  (L249-L260) emits one `foot_{index}` part and one `frame_to_foot_{index}` FIXED joint per station
  in a station list, and nothing in the loop body depends on the station being one of four. The
  template re-derives the station list from the base footprint, so N=4 reproduces the source's
  rectangle of corner pads and other N values walk the same perimeter.
* `bench_clamp_bracket` — the clamp base builds a MIRRORED PAIR of identical triangular gusset ribs
  across the throat-to-plate junction (L64-L82), the same solid at two x stations. The template
  generalises that pair to N ribs evenly spaced across the throat wall. Note that the source built
  2 and the template's declared minimum is 3, i.e. N is generalised UPWARD here, not truncated.

Nothing in the pool was given up for compile budget: the largest declared combination is 12 parts
and 11 joints (6 feet + frame + cup + strainer + ram + lever + linkage), inside the fleet's
19-part p90.

Deliberate deviations from source construction, recorded here because they change how a cited span
is reproduced:

* `rack_pinion_crank` — the source authors `SpurGear(module=0.002, ...)` and
  `RackGear(module=0.002, ...)` at METRE scale. OCC's absolute tolerances make a metre-scale
  involute gear degenerate (non-watertight, tessellating into disconnected components, and prone to
  SIGSEGV when a bore is cut against involute faces). The template builds the same gear pair in
  MILLIMETRES and meshes it with `unit_scale=0.001`, and cuts the pinion bore itself instead of
  passing `bore_d` to `build()`. Same mechanism, same module/teeth relation, valid solid.
* Every record in the pool wraps its mechanism interfaces in `ctx.allow_overlap` (7 calls in the
  C-frame fork, 12 in the rack-and-pinion fork). That call is blocked for Design-backed templates.
  The template instead cuts the real openings the source only asserted — a bored lever bushing, a
  rack/pinion window and shaft bore through the beam, a bored nut housing, a bored clamp jaw — and
  sizes every bore as a running fit under 1 mm, so each member is carried by its own bearing
  instead of by an allowance. `ctx.allow_overlap` is never called.
* `c_arch_throat` mounts the lever pivot on a bearing lug on the throat arm's FRONT face instead
  of on the column front face above the arm (source `c_frame_lever_refill` L423-L438, pivot at
  `(-0.040, 0.052, 0.480)`). The source placement drives both the lever arm and the dog-link
  straight through the 58 mm-wide throat arm — four of its seven allowances exist only to suppress
  that, and its own comment says a production casting "would have a guide slot there". A slot wide
  enough for the link through an arm that narrow separates the ram boss from the column, so the
  honest resolution is to put the mechanism plane ahead of the beam, which is exactly what the
  twin-post origin already does (arm plane y = -0.054 against a beam front face at y = -0.021).
  Every other C-arch fact is kept: one welded body, a rear column set to clear the cup rim, a
  forward throat arm, the guide boss, a single bore through arm and boss, and the column standing
  proud of the arm.
* Every joint limit is DERIVED from a real stop rather than copied. The source's fixed 0.080 /
  0.055 vessel lifts drive the rising cup rim and strainer plate straight into the lowered press
  head, so the removal travel is taken out of the same tip-to-plate clearance budget the stroke
  uses (worst coupled pose leaves 3 mm of tip contact, inside the 5 mm gate); its fixed 2.86 rad
  pinion sweep carries the crank grip back through the beam envelope at the fine end of the module
  range, so the sweep is `travel / pitch_radius` with the pitch radius floored at `travel / 2`;
  its fixed -32 deg lever up-limit swings the dog-link's lower leg — the only member that reaches
  back behind the beam face to find the ram collar — into the beam underside on short-stack
  seeds, so the up-limit is solved from where that leg would bottom on the beam. The lever's
  down-limit likewise comes from the grip tip clearing the bench.
* The gusset ribs move from under the mounting plate (source L64-L82, where they sit inside the
  volume the bench slab occupies, and where one of the pair lands partly outside the plate) to the
  outboard face of the throat wall, tying wall to jaw entirely clear of the throat. Same element,
  real load path, and it is what makes the rib count a usable multiplicity.
