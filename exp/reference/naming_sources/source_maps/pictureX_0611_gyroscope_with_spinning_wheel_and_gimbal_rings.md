category_scope: One demonstration gyroscope whose root is a grounded or wall-fixed support that carries a nested stack of gimbal rings on orthogonal trunnion axes, with a spinning flywheel rotor captured in the innermost ring on a spin axis that passes through the common gimbal centre. Every rotation axis in scope passes through that one centre. In scope: the support (weighted pedestal, slender column, tripod, wall bracket), the gimbal ring cross-section and count, the way the innermost ring captures the rotor spindle, the rotor/flywheel form, the outer-ring pole hardware, and whether the gimbal swing is free or mechanically limited by end stops. Out of scope: bearings and flywheels that are not carried in a gimbal (bench grinders, fans, potter's wheels), inertial navigation boxes with no visible rings, toy tops with no gimbal, and gyroscopic exercise balls. The two origin records are the PNG-anchored reviews of `001.png` and `002.png`; the eight `rec_picturex0611_gyroscope_var_*` records are forked structural variants of those two anchors and every one was read in full.

## Deliberate deviations from the source pool

These are recorded here so the implementation can be audited against them.

1. **Bores instead of overlap allowances.** Every source record expresses a shaft-in-bearing fit as two intersecting solids wrapped in `ctx.allow_overlap`, which is blocked for Design-backed templates. The rebuild cuts a real bore through every bearing body and runs each pin, trunnion and axle in it on a 0.5 mm radial running clearance, so the fit is supported by the baseline QC contact rule instead of an allowance.
2. **The axle stays inside the innermost ring's envelope.** Family A's 205 mm axle leaves the outer ring; the source only survives it because its rest pose keeps the tilt joint far from zero, and the three-ring fork has to `allow_overlap` the axle through the middle ring. The rebuild caps the axle at the innermost ring's bearing faces and keeps the record's long-axle silhouette cue as a spindle cap plus an axial counterweight inside the ring.
3. **Every ring plane is derived from the two axes that ring carries.** `three_axis_gimbal` draws its middle ring in the XZ plane while pivoting it about X and providing Y, and `wall_bracket` pivots the outer hoop about its own normal. The rebuild always places a ring's plane so that it contains its own trunnion axis, and puts a spindle carrier's plane either through the spin axis (`opposed_bearings`) or perpendicular to it (`bearing_spider`), which is exactly the two arrangements the two anchors actually use.
4. **Ring count is carried as a structural slot, not a multiplicity.** The third ring is not a repeat of the second: it adds a distinct fourth rotation axis and re-orients every ring plane in the stack, so it is accounted as a `ring_stack` candidate. The genuinely repeated element, the rotor's radial rim members, is the multiplicity.
5. **Nothing was given up for compile budget.** The finished object is 4 or 5 parts and 3 or 4 joints, far inside the fleet budget, so no source structure had to be dropped.
