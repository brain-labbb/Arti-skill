Model a vintage playground seesaw, about 3.0 m long and 0.8 m tall at the pivot. The base is two arched legs made of bent galvanized steel tube (~50 mm diameter), heavily weathered with rust patches; the two arches cross side by side forming an A-shaped saddle whose apex carries the pivot. The rocking beam is a single rectangular steel bar (~80 mm wide, 40 mm thick) painted mustard yellow with extensive rust streaks. Each end of the beam carries a small flat wooden seat plate, an inverted-U steel grab handle (~0.25 m tall bent rod) just inboard of the seat, and a curved black rubber tire-section bumper bolted under the tip to cushion ground impact. A triangular gusset plate connects the beam to the pivot. Articulation: the beam attaches to the apex of the arched base with a single revolute joint whose axis is horizontal and perpendicular to the beam length, allowing the seesaw to rock about plus/minus 20 degrees from level so each end alternately nears the ground. Base arches are static. Keep tubes as swept cylinders, beam as a long box, bumpers as partial-cylinder shells. Reference image: picture/Playground/Seesaw/001.png
